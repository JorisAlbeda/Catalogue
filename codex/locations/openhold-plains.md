# Plains (Openhold)

## Description

Open country in the Provinces, unnamed on any older map — empty ground nobody at court had ever bothered to claim or call anything, until Lilith Woodborne stumbled onto it and named it herself, for lack of anything better. Marren Wick gave it a real name once the keep and the town around it were hers to name: Openhold, after the ledger she swore to keep open here, over the throne, over everything else.

## History

Discovered by Lilith Woodborne while she was campaigning through the Provinces. She built it up as a personal holding, trading with the itinerant Wild Cry and the traders of Tinker's Fair, and kept two of her prizes here — the Circlet of Command and the Darkest Secret — behind only a handful of guards.

Marren Wick broke those guards here, took the ring from Lilith's own head, and forced open the chest Ivan had been keeping close since Magician's Code. What was inside — the tome that tells how to conjure the Darkest Flame — has been hers since. She's held the ground rather than withdraw with it, growing her own following of traders and recruits on the same open country the Chancellor once claimed for her own.

An old, half-fallen keep stands on the ground as well, long squatted in by a bandit clan. Marren drove them out and set a stonemason to rebuilding it — the first ground she's ever ruled outright, not inherited or claimed by title.

Rowan Voss brought Ashvale's own column against that keep in the fourth month of the war, reaching for the ring itself. The walls held. She withdrew a warband short of what she'd marched with, and left Tamsin Reed behind for good.

In the years after the war's end, the ground took the name Openhold — Marren Wick's own choosing, and the first name this place ever had that wasn't just a placeholder for whoever happened to be standing on it. The town growing up around the keep, and the wider country answering to it, are known together now as the Openlands: no throne, no Empire, just the ledger Marren swore to keep open, and whoever's willing to be judged by the same one she is.
