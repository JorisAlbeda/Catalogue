# Salt Flats

## Description

A wide, flat stretch of dried salt-crust and pale sand in the Provinces, bare enough that a rider can be seen crossing it from a mile off in any direction. A single river cuts along one edge of it — the only green thing for a long walk in any direction, until this season.

## History

Bandit-held for as long as anyone still living remembers ruling it otherwise. Round 0: Teagan Sedge and her Ashvale companies arrived to reclaim ground her family lost here three generations back, and made camp within sight of the bandit banner still holding it.

Round 0: a scout found Tinney Woodborne — a self-declared emissary of the Old Oak — singing to the riverbank, coaxing a shrub and then a handful of raspberries out of ground that had grown nothing but salt in living memory. Tinney spoke of a coming change, "the time has finally come for us all to rejoin nature," and left wading up the river toward Openhold, meaning to tell the Truthwatcher that the city itself would become forest again. Whatever it means, the ground here answered Tinney's voice in a way it's never answered anyone else's.

Round 1: Teagan Sedge broke the bandit hold on the ground outright, throwing her full company at the camp in one push. It cost her two of the three warbands she'd marched from Ashvale with, but the banner came down before noon. She stayed to hold what was left of it herself rather than send anyone else in her place.

Round 1, later the same day: searching among whoever the bandits had been keeping, Teagan found Sela Brand still working her old trade at the riverside, and freed an innkeeper, Farrin Dale, put to stretching rations for his captors. Both stayed on. Word that Salt Flats now answers to a paying hand rather than a bandit camp drew two more recruits to Teagan's banner before the day was out.

## Location

Provinces

## Garrison

The Farrow Company — what's left of the force Teagan Sedge led out of Ashvale, one warband strong after the assault that took this ground, named for Aleyn Farrow, who never made it out of the watchtower this company lost that same spring. Teagan holds it herself, in person, rather than hand it to anyone else — grim, stripped down, and answering to no one's authority but hers.
