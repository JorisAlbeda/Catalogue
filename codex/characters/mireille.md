# Mireille

## Description

A wandering teller of half-true tales, wrapped in a patched cloak too warm for most of the roads she walks, wild dark hair kept out of her face by nothing more than habit. Talks with her hands as much as her voice, and has never once told the same story the same way twice — not because she forgets, she says, but because the story's never finished with her yet.

## History

Made her living the old way, town to town, trading a night's telling for a bed and a meal, filling in whatever a place didn't already know about itself with whatever she'd picked up three towns back. Learned early that a story people already half-believe is worth more kept polished than one nobody's heard — the Darkest Secret's own name came up often enough on her circuit that she started collecting every version of it she crossed, less out of ambition than habit, the same way she collected everything else.

Found her way onto Teagan Sedge's own road at Narrow Pass, another traveler passing through a crossing nobody used to talk about either. Took the offer to stay without much persuading — a captain rebuilding a name is exactly the kind of story worth being close to when it's still being written.

Stayed on at what's now Farrowhold long past the point any other stop would have held her — decades, in the end, though she'd never put a number on it herself. Got her ending for the story she'd been collecting since before Teagan ever offered her a chair: the captain, the horn, the last conversation with the weapon-maker that never got a second one. Still tells it, when asked, though she's stopped promising it's true.

## Location

Farrowhold, Provinces

## Status

Active — older now, mostly settled at Farrowhold rather than the road,
though she still tells a story to anyone passing through who'll sit
still for one.

## Voice

Vocabulary level: rich and digressive — reaches for a comparison or a half-remembered tale before a plain answer, and rarely apologizes for the detour. Verbal habit: prefaces a claim with where she heard it rather than whether it's true ("They say, three towns back..."), and treats her own certainty as beside the point. Emotional default: curious rather than guarded — treats even bad news as material first, feeling second. Speech rhythm: unhurried, circular, willing to double back to an earlier point rather than finish in order.
