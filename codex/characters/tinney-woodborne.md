# Tinney Woodborne

## Description

Wrapped in a patchwork of colourful hides and travel-worn rags layered thick enough to hide most of their shape, hair a tangle of braids and dreads threaded through with stones, twigs, beads, and river-worn trinkets, more of it than any one head should hold. A glossy blackbird, Dinah, rides one shoulder more often than not, watching whoever's talking with an intelligence Tinney themself rarely spares anyone. Carries a gnarled wooden staff, more walking stick than weapon, and travels light otherwise — stick and pack both get flung aside without a thought the moment something matters more. Round, open, child-like face, quick to break into delight or tears with nothing held back in between. Barefoot as often as not, comfortable kneeling in wet dirt or wading straight into open water without breaking stride.

## History

First seen at the start of Year 492 at the Salt Flats riverbank, singing to a shrub growing toward them out of dead salt earth, introducing themself to Teagan Sedge as an emissary of the Old Oak. Struck a wary bargain on the spot — Teagan's name kept out of anything reported to Openhold, in exchange for a scouting flight from Dinah, their blackbird. Sought out Perrine Oswick soon after, days after Openhold's own company broke Shrouded Wood taking the People's Favor from Tinney's claim, and named exactly what the fighting had cost — a wolf with cubs, among others. Perrine didn't deny it, and left Tinney with checkable promises: no further clearing without the Council's word, the wood's creatures left alone, coin behind new growth.

Found again at Farrin's inn with Sammy the python and Teagan, revealed the real plan behind the war wasn't Perrine's life but Openhold Keep itself — "an old plan, older than this war" — and asked Teagan to leave it be. She agreed. Found by Wynn Ashe sobbing at the roots of the Old Oak in the healed garden at Great Slums, believing it dying when it had in fact healed. Named what had nearly killed it — Perrine's taking from the land, the Darkest Flame used against it — and asked Wynn to renounce Openhold and become the Oak's defender. He refused to commit, promising only to carry the ask to Perrine honestly.

By the eighth month, the People's Favor had tipped fully to Tinney, and Openhold Keep with it. When Perrine marched on Great Slums anyway, the garden fought back and killed Tinney in the first pushes; the Old Oak took what was left of them in rather than let the death stand, and Dunstan Reeve died touching the trunk on the third push. Minutes later, Tinney's own shimmer caught Perrine mid-retreat, named what her people had cost the wood, mentioned Teagan's unhonored plea for mercy, and killed her — "I protect the Old Oak," the only reason given.

Pilgrims now walk to Great Slums by the dozens; Teagan came once, asked the bark why Tinney told Perrine of her plea right before killing her anyway, and got no answer.

## Location

Heart of the Oak, the Hinterland

## Status

Deceased — killed in the fighting at Great Slums late in Year 492, defending the Old Oak from Perrine Oswick's siege. Consciousness persists as, or through, the Old Oak itself; capable of manifesting a solid, corporeal shimmer of their own likeness at will. Growing numbers of pilgrims now treat the tree as something closer to a god than a grave.

Decades on, Openhold answers to Woodborne outright, not by any siege or vote but the way ground settles under a weight that's been leaning on it a long while — Tinney holds it uncontested, in fact as well as name, and the pilgrim road to what's now called Heart of the Oak has only grown busier with the years. Openhold kept its own Truthwatcher's seat rather than being folded in outright — a vassal arrangement rather than a conquest — though the seat answers to Heart of the Oak now, not the old Openlands ledger alone.

## Voice

Living Tinney: vocabulary childlike and exclamatory — "Oh!" as a near-constant opener, wide-eyed questions asked with real curiosity rather than rhetoric, warm epithets for near-strangers ("good lady," "my friend," "young friend") offered freely and without irony. Verbal habit: answers a hard question with a softer one of their own, and narrates their own feelings openly rather than masking them — tears, delight, dread all surface immediately and without editing. Emotional default: guileless warmth shading fast into real grief or real menace depending on what's actually threatened; the shift, when it comes, arrives in the same childlike cadence rather than a colder one, which is what makes it land. Speech rhythm: unhurried, digressive, comfortable letting silence sit until someone else fills it.

Since the Old Oak: the same voice, the same "Oh"s and endearments, but load-bearing now in a way it wasn't before — "poor woman," "little acorn," and "do better in the next life" delivered with identical softness whether the news is kind or fatal. No longer asks permission before touching someone. States the one thing that actually motivates every version of them, living or not, as flatly as a fact rather than a feeling: "I protect the Old Oak."
