# Perrine Oswick

## Description

Perrine Oswick kept a slim, closed ledger with her always, and a measuring cord at her belt that had been her mother's before it was hers, worn smooth at the knot. Ink stains sat permanently grey-black in the creases of her hands. She dressed plainly, in a dark buttoned coat cut for use rather than court, and carried herself like a woman used to being watched by neighbours who'd known her family for three generations — which she was. By a reputation earned over a decade on the road, she was incapable of letting a lie stand unchallenged, even a small, kind one.

## History

Born inside Openhold's own walls to Joss Oswick, the town's exacting granary-ledger keeper, and Ede, who ran Tinker's Fair's mending stall and taught Perrine to read a scale as unbendingly. Spent a decade after riding circuit as a tally-rider, settling boundary disputes across Cradle with nothing but her mother's cord and a reputation that never bent. Took up the Truthwatcher's seat meaning to finish what every predecessor since Marren Wick had left undone: the two stretches of ground past Salt Flats no Truthwatcher had ever claimed. Meant to do it by opening an honest, standing account for the bandit clans already holding it, rather than march on them the way Marren once had.

Wynn Ashe's own Campaign against Teagan Sedge, fought through Vow of Union's loophole, pulled her garrison from Shrouded Wood without her leave and left him holding a book with the standing to unseat her office. She told him so at the Openhold Plains, then rode on Buried Giant herself a month later and took the book back by force rather than wait for an answer. Word reached her that Tinney Woodborne's claim over Openhold had grown strong enough to hand them the realm outright. Wynn asked her to leave the healed garden at Great Slums be; she couldn't, and marched on it regardless while he went to ask Tinney himself one more time.

She reached Great Slums to find the ground itself turned against her — spores, vines, Tinney's own voice speaking from the bark. Three pushes broke against the tree; Dunstan Reeve died on the third, spear still in hand. Mid-retreat her own feet stopped answering her, and Tinney's shimmer closed the distance, named what her people had cost the wood, and killed her with a hand to the throat — "protect the Old Oak" the only reason given.

What's remembered of her at Openhold isn't the desk or the title — it's the cord at her belt, and the habit that went with it: re-opening a settled account rather than letting a wrong number sit, whoever it cost her to say so.

## Location

Great Slums, Provinces

## Status

Deceased — killed by Tinney Woodborne at Great Slums, late in Year 492 of the Old Oak, the same season Tinney's own claim over Openhold went uncontested for good.

## Voice

Vocabulary level: plain and exact — reaches for a specific number or place-name before an adjective every time. Verbal habit: repeats back whatever she's just been told, word for word, before she'll answer it — a tally-rider's old habit of confirming a claim before she's willing to act on it. Emotional default: steady and watchful; rarely raises her voice, even when naming a lie outright. Speech rhythm: measured, unhurried, willing to let a silence sit rather than fill it with something she hasn't checked yet.

**Plain means short.** Sentences of five to ten words, not twenty-five. She states the fact and stops — "I'm not leaving Great Slums alone," not "I'm not leaving Great Slums alone, not because I lack the nerve to spare it but because the account I've been keeping won't let me pretend otherwise." No hedge clauses ("not because," "rather than," "not as an excuse for"). No narrating her own honesty before delivering it ("I need you to hear this plainly" is not plain — just say the thing). One idea per sentence. If a line needs two commas to finish a single clause, cut it in half or cut the second half entirely.
