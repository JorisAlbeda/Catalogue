# Rowan Voss

## Description

Rowan Voss wears a travel-worn, mud-hemmed cloak cut in the old Ashvale style, cinched with a wide belt and satchel. Where the paintings show Perrin Ashe's hands still and empty at his sides, refusing what was offered, Rowan's don't stay still — she reaches, touches, closes around whatever's put in front of her before she's decided whether to keep it. She holds the Warden's office by appointment, not blood, and it shows: she reaches for proof she's earned it — oaths, relics, small formalities Perrin himself never bothered with. Quick to act, slower to trust her own right to act.

## History

Ashvale's Wardens have always stood for election by the ruling line rather than inheriting the office outright — but every Warden since Perrin has been an Ashe by blood, the vote a formality more than a real choice. Rowan Voss is the first it's gone to who isn't. She set out from Ashvale as its newest Exile as word spread of a new war brewing in the Woodland Empire, wanting to know firsthand whether the office Perrin Ashe built by refusing the Horned Mask still means what it meant a century ago, or whether it was only ever his excuse to walk away. She arrived at Drowned City, where she recruited the Truthwatcher Maren Cole as her adviser, at the very start of that campaign.

By the third month of the war, ruling the Great Slum in the Hinterland (the Marshes, it later turned out, she'd only ever half-claimed), Rowan turned down Chancellor Lilith Woodborne's offer of shared defense against Marren Wick and Iona Ashe — twice by messenger, then once to Lilith's face at the Marshes. Lilith called her selfish outright and rode off; Rowan let the word stand rather than argue it.

By the fourth month of the war, with Marren Wick close to an outright win, Rowan pulled every warband she could gather out of the Marshes and marched south for Plains itself, aiming to strip the one thing standing unguarded between Marren and her throne: the Circlet of Command.

Rowan came home from that war with no throne, no relic, and no victory to her name, and spent the rest of her life telling anyone who asked that the empty hands were the real answer. She kept a gull-grey scarf, mended twice, in the Warden's own strongbox rather than wear or give it away — Tamsin Reed's, left behind at Plains. She died an old woman, long enough ago that her great-granddaughter Teagan Sedge now only knows her through family story.
