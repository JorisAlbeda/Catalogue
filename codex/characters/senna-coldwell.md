# Senna Coldwell

## Description

Broad-shouldered and grey early, the way people who've spent thirty years drilling in Openhold's wet mornings tend to go. Carries an old, oath-notched spearhead on a thong at her collar instead of any court medallion — not the shaft, just the head, pulled from a weapon sworn true and worn to uselessness before she was ever born. Answers a direct question with a flat yes or no before she lets herself explain it, a habit the Wallwatch never quite drilled out of her, though the desk keeps trying.

## History

Apprenticed at nine to Openhold's own granary scales, the way half the town still is — hated the arithmetic of it, not the numbers, the certainty a ledger claims to have that a person testing a blade never gets to. At sixteen she swore herself to the Wallwatch instead, over a mother's ledger and a mother's objections both, into a company that had gone twenty-some years without anyone willing to actually lead it — Dunstan Reeve dead at Great Slums the same year Perrine Oswick was, the post handed down to no one since. She picked the gap up a piece at a time under the sergeant who'd called the retreat over what was left of Dunstan, and who told her exactly what that tree did to him, once, plainly, the way he told her everything.

Perrine Oswick's own desk went the same way the Wallwatch's command did — unclaimed, this time by nobody at Heart of the Oak troubling to fill it, forty-eight years running. Senna took the Wallwatch's own command openly this year, the first to hold it since Dunstan, and the Truthwatcher's desk within the same season — not because Openhold asked her to, exactly, but because nobody with better standing was going to, and she'd rather it be someone who still tests a blade before she trusts it than someone who never has.

Took Cade Marrow, lord of Oathforge, prisoner at Ancient City in Year 540, along with the Darkest Flame he'd been carrying — the week's chase cost her the better part of a company first. She got Squint's intercepted letter to use as leverage, and used it plainly rather than in secret — the same discipline she's holding the fire itself to.

Held him a further week rather than march him home untested, asking the same question every morning and refusing him every morning in turn, until she came to him wanting the truth about the fire more than she wanted to keep refusing it. He gave it to her plainly, hammer bound to her Wallwatch's iron the same as he'd offered Fia, and asked for his freedom outright rather than another day proving himself. She freed him on the spot — one honest answer standing in for the season of testing she'd have held anyone else to, the first time since taking the desk that she's trusted something before it passed her own usual measure of it.

What she asked for in that same bargain took two months to arrive and reads differently once it has: the vow bound Cade's hammer to her Wallwatch's iron, and an oath-sworn blade is the one kind that bites true on Empire ground where plain steel goes dull. She commissioned it in the same week she took the Darkest Flame off him and swore to Fia she'd never kill with it — arming herself against the Emissary's own forces with one hand while giving her word with the other, whether or not she had named that to herself yet.
