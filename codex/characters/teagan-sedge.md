# Teagan Sedge

## Description

Calloused hands and a long scar along one forearm from a raider's blade that didn't quite miss. Wears scavenged Ashvale watch-company leathers over her own company's plain grey sash, a fire-scorched signal-horn — pulled from her watchtower's ashes this spring — slung at her hip in place of the one that burned with it. Watches a room the way she watches a tree line: for whoever hasn't moved yet.

## History

Born to a family three generations displaced from Salt Flats, she grew up in Ashvale's watchtower camps and was conscripted as a girl into the labor gangs rebuilding the border forts, rising over the years to captain the very company that held them. Family stories still told of her great-grandmother Rowan Voss, once Ashvale's own Warden, who marched on the Woodland Empire chasing some grand answer and came home with nothing to show for it but the conviction that reaching for a throne wasn't worth what it cost. Teagan grew up doubting that lesson.

This spring put it to the test. The same bandit banner that's squatted on Salt Flats since her family fled pushed a raid deep enough to burn her own watchtower to the frame, and cost her Aleyn Farrow, her second-in-command and oldest friend in the company, who held the gate alone so the rest could fall back. Teagan didn't fall back afterward, and hasn't since. She's done patching the symptom: two generations of Wardens and Truthwatchers have let Ashvale's whole western edge bleed while Openhold prospers untouched, and she means to burn that arrangement down at the root — retaking Salt Flats herself, and then Openhold's own throne, rather than hold one more watchtower for a Truthwatcher who's never once ridden out to see what's actually happening past Cradle.

She marched on the bandit banner squatting Salt Flats that same spring of Year 492, and took it back in one committed push — the cost was steep, two of her three warbands, but the ground was hers now, not borrowed. She renamed what was left of her company the Farrow Company before the day was out.

Within the month she took the Openhold Plains outright from Openhold itself — bribed her way past Openhold Keep's own gate, then broke the Wallwatch in the fight that followed, losing more of her own company than the tower or the flats had ever cost her. Second ground under her own banner, the first ever taken from Openhold rather than from bandits.

She lost it all again before the season turned. Wynn Ashe's siege broke that same gate, the Farrow Company under Ren Ashby died defending it, and Teagan herself was taken alive once the Darkest Flame ended the fight — the tome she'd spent the season chasing went with Wynn when he withdrew. She didn't stay a prisoner long: bandits overran the emptied ground two days later, and she slipped free in the confusion, with nothing left to her name but the company at Salt Flats and whatever she could rebuild from it.

She walked back to Salt Flats rather than contest the still bandit-held Openhold Plains, and called in fresh hands to the Farrow Company there instead — the first step of rebuilding from the one ground she still held outright.

Days after Openhold's own fall that same year, she found Wynn Ashe and what was left of his company at a tavern table, and sat down across from him without asking — needling him first about Openhold's own end before she'd even heard the account, the two of them the last survivors left from this whole war by her own count. Got the whole of it: Dunstan Reeve's three broken pushes into Great Slums, Tinney turned into something that answered out of the tree itself, a borrowed voice that named her own plea for Perrine's life and let it go unanswered anyway. Asked the one question that mattered to her — would he light the Darkest Flame again, the same fire that killed Ren Ashby — and he said yes, on the same terms, no softening it. She named him for it: not a man who'd hold a gate twice, a weapon that fires the same regardless of who's standing across from it next. When he reached for his hold as the reason, she turned the whole account back on him — the dead, the fallen realm, whether he'd once stopped to consider he might have been wrong. He said he'd tried, talked both Tinney and Perrine down before either fight started. She didn't take it as an answer. Left him with a look of pity rather than a last word, Mireille falling in behind her without being told to.

She never took Openhold's own throne, and once Tinney Woodborne settled into it root and bark, she never knelt to that one either. Went back to Salt Flats and made it a country in its own right rather than a holding waiting on somebody else's ledger — Farrowhold, she named it, after the friend who'd died holding a gate so the rest of the company could fall back. A throne was still a throne to her, whatever it was rooted in.

Decades on: held Salt Flats the rest of her working life, never took Openhold's own throne and never knelt to whoever sat it either — the answer she landed on was narrower than her great-grandmother Rowan's, not a flat no to thrones, just no kneeling to one. Handed the Farrow Company to hands she trusted once her knees stopped trusting her, but never handed over the horn, and never spoke Wynn Ashe's name at Salt Flats again. The Darkest Secret still sits behind her tower gate, kept rather than spent.

## Location

Salt Flats, Provinces

## Status

Active — old now, ruling Farrowhold in name more than in daily
business, having long since handed the Farrow Company to hands she
trusts. Still to be found at Salt Flats: the country's own elder, and
a first-hand account of the war for anyone who asks rather than a
story handed down secondhand.

## Voice

Vocabulary level: simple — field-command language, no flourish, no rank she hasn't earned herself. Verbal habits: gives even ordinary requests as flat declaratives, never as questions ("Get water," not "could you get water"); when genuinely rattled, goes quiet and counts something under her breath instead of speaking — paces, torches, whatever's in front of her. Emotional default: guarded and coiled, calm because she's decided to be, not because she is. Speech rhythm: clipped, short sentences — the one exception is Aleyn Farrow's name, which she starts and doesn't finish.
