# Tib

## Description

Knee-high and green-thumbed, with a needle carried crosswise in her teeth the way a carter carries a nail, and a coat pieced together out of scraps too small to have come off anything in particular. Unimpressive in every direction at once. Sits closer to a fire than she is invited to and mends whatever is nearest to hand while she talks, whether or not it needed mending.

## Voice

Talks in small practical instructions — *hold that, turn it, don't pull* — and treats enormous consequences as though they were housekeeping. Never explains what a thing means; explains only what to do with it. Says "there" when she has finished something.

## History

Nobody has established where Tib came from, and she treats the question as rude in the way a question about somebody's wages is rude. What is known is what she makes. A favour, in the old sense — a ribbon, a scrap, a thumb's width of stitching given to somebody about to go somewhere dangerous — is a small thing to make and a permanent thing to have made, and Tib has been making them for longer than any of the Provinces' own record-keeping goes back.

She does not give them for payment and cannot be induced to make one on request. She gives them for courtesies, and the courtesies she accepts are almost insultingly small: a place at a fire, a light, leave to sit out a rain. What she gives back is thread, and everyone alive still wearing her thread is, in a sense the fae find obvious and Farrowhold finds alarming, walking the same road.

Marek Sten gave her a seat at his fire out past the wood without looking up from what he was doing. She stitched a thumb's width of bad green thread to his sleeve, told him to try not to lose it, and inside the week four companies who had worn her work for generations came up out of the long grass and the standing water to walk where he was walking. She has not explained this. She has mended his coat twice since and offered no further comment on any of it.
