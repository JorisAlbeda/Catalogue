# Perrine Oswick

## Description

Perrine Oswick keeps a slim, closed ledger with her always, and a measuring cord at her belt that was her mother's before it was hers, worn smooth at the knot. Ink stains sit permanently grey-black in the creases of her hands. She dresses plainly, in a dark buttoned coat cut for use rather than court, and carries herself like a woman used to being watched by neighbours who've known her family for three generations — which she is. By a reputation earned over a decade on the road, she is incapable of letting a lie stand unchallenged, even a small, kind one.

## History

Perrine was born inside Openhold's own walls, which haven't moved since Marren Wick raised them eighty-some years ago — the Hold has only grown inward since, denser each generation, until Tinker's Fair's stalls stand close enough to touch and everyone's ledger is common knowledge whether they like it or not. Her father, Joss Oswick, kept the town's granary ledgers, exacting to a fault, the kind of honest that won't round a measure even when a crowd wants him to. Her mother, Ede, ran the mending stall at Tinker's Fair and taught Perrine to read a scale the same honest way, warmer than Joss's but no less unbending. Perrine spent a decade after that riding circuit as a tally-rider, settling boundary disputes across Cradle with nothing but her mother's cord and a reputation that never bent.

She took up the Truthwatcher's seat meaning to finish something that outlived Marren Wick herself: the two stretches of ground past Salt Flats that every Truthwatcher since has left unclaimed, including Bevan Stroud, who held this desk for the thirty-one years before Perrine and coined the phrase Openhold still repeats — that the ledger records the world and doesn't go looking for more of it. Perrine means to prove him wrong without repeating Marren's own method: not by marching on that ground, the way Marren took this desk in the first place, but by opening an honest, standing account for the bandit clans already holding it — a real stake in Openhold's trade, tallied and paid out exactly as promised — trusting her own audited reputation to succeed where a throne's word never has. She's turned over the old story of the Bandit Crown more than once, the relic that once let bandits ally with the Empire outright under Lilianne with no war at all, unrecovered since and unplaced on any map — and wonders whether it's still sitting somewhere in the same unclaimed ground she means to go see for herself.

Wynn Ashe's own Campaign against Teagan Sedge, fought with Vow of Union's own loophole, pulled Perrine's own garrison out of Shrouded Wood without her leave and spent it in full — and left Wynn holding a book with the standing to unseat her own office by rule alone if the season turned against her. She told him so to his face at the edge of the Openhold Plains, named his age and the clause both, and rode away with the question of what to do about it genuinely unresolved. A month later she rode on Buried Giant itself instead of waiting on an answer that might never come — no warning, no Scepter, just her own terms put to him directly. The Cairn Watch he set against her turned out to answer to the Hold, not to him, same as it always had; it didn't hold, and she took the book back with her own hands rather than his home.

Word reached her camp at Salt Flats that Tinney Woodborne had taken the People's Favor back, and worse, that the banner had grown rich enough to hand Tinney the whole realm outright within a single day if it stayed in their hands. Wynn rode in that same evening asking her to leave a healed garden past Great Slums be — the very ground the banner now sat on. She couldn't grant it. Told him plainly what was actually at stake, and he offered the only thing he had left to give without breaking his own word to Tinney or raising a blade against them: going back once more, to ask them himself. She sent him, and marched for Great Slums regardless, not waiting to find out whether his asking would be enough.

She reached Great Slums to find the ground itself turned against her — the garden grown wild through the streets, biting spores dropping soldiers where they stood, and Tinney's own voice speaking out of the tree's bark when the fighting turned against them. Three separate pushes, each one further than the last, each one broken before it reached the banner. Dunstan Reeve died on the third, spear still in his hand, killed the instant he touched the tree. She held the field only long enough to watch the retreat sound. The banner never moved. She rode out with two warbands of the Wallwatch left standing and nothing bought for the cost.

She never made it past the Slums' own edge. Mid-retreat, her own feet stopped answering her — rooted where she stood while the rest of the line broke around her — and a shimmer resolved into Tinney Woodborne's own shape, translucent at first, solid and warm by the time their hand reached her cheek. They named what her people had cost the wood and the Flame she'd already told them wasn't hers, and she corrected both, plainly, even with her legs locked under her. Named, too, that Teagan Sedge had asked Tinney to spare her — a debt she died not understanding. The hand moved to her throat before she'd finished asking why. Tinney said only that they protect the Old Oak, pressed their brow to hers as the light went, and told her to do better in the next life. She didn't get another word out. ## Status

## Voice

Vocabulary level: plain and exact — reaches for a specific number or place-name before an adjective every time. Verbal habit: repeats back whatever she's just been told, word for word, before she'll answer it — a tally-rider's old habit of confirming a claim before she's willing to act on it. Emotional default: steady and watchful; rarely raises her voice, even when naming a lie outright. Speech rhythm: measured, unhurried, willing to let a silence sit rather than fill it with something she hasn't checked yet.

**Plain means short.** Sentences of five to ten words, not twenty-five. She states the fact and stops — "I'm not leaving Great Slums alone," not "I'm not leaving Great Slums alone, not because I lack the nerve to spare it but because the account I've been keeping won't let me pretend otherwise." No hedge clauses ("not because," "rather than," "not as an excuse for"). No narrating her own honesty before delivering it ("I need you to hear this plainly" is not plain — just say the thing). One idea per sentence. If a line needs two commas to finish a single clause, cut it in half or cut the second half entirely.
