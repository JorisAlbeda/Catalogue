# Wynn Ashe

## Description

Wynn Ashe is old now, sixty-seven, hands slower than they were but
still chalk-marked at the seams the way a mason's always are. He still
carries the same folded page, ink long gone soft at the creases, in
the same inside pocket out of habit more than need these days. Still
counts a debt unsettled the same way the old family stories say Iona
Ashe always did — a debt over a century old now, and by his own
reckoning still not paid, though he no longer expects to be the one
who pays it.

White hair, cropped short out of the same old practicality rather than
vanity. A weathered, deep-lined face, watchful grey eyes gone a little
paler with age but still slow to blink first, and the same thin pale
scar through one eyebrow from a chisel that slipped when he was
sixteen. Walks with a stick now, more for the bad knee than the
balance.

## History

Grew up in an Ashvale tavern that had kept Iona Ashe's coat by its hearth for generations, and with it the family habit of never calling a debt settled just because it had gone quiet. At sixteen, apprenticed as a mason, he took his turn re-cutting the worn memorial at the Great Spire, the ground where the Darkest Flame lay buried. When the tower came down at the war's start and Buried Giant fell to bandits and relic-hunters, he raised a company of Ashvale volunteers, the Cairn Watch, and marched them there himself at nineteen with Yarrow Doss at his side. He recovered the Darkest Flame from the tower's own rubble, and held the ground the following year when Perrine Oswick rode on Buried Giant demanding the book that gave her the standing to unseat her own office; she took the book back regardless, but left his claim on the ground untouched.

He found the healed garden at Great Slums before Tinney Woodborne did, and kept it to himself. When Tinney asked him to renounce Openhold outright, he refused — but rode to Perrine that same week asking her to leave the garden be, and told her the Flame at the Keep had been his hand, not hers. When neither ask held, he went back to Tinney a second time rather than fight or let Perrine march in blind, then watched Perrine's Campaign break against that garden from cover — Dunstan Reeve's death, Tinney's own death and rebirth into the Old Oak, Perrine's death at Tinney's borrowed hand.

Days later, Teagan Sedge found him and made him give the whole account, then asked if he'd light the Flame again knowing the cost. He said yes. She named him for it and walked away. He never went home to Ashvale. Spent the decades since holding the ground he'd marched to take, re-cutting the memorial each time it wore down, and burying Yarrow, Corin, and Dagny himself when their own time came. Old now, sixty-seven, still at Buried Giant, with a fresh copy of his own folded page left at the tavern hearth for the day he can't tell it himself.

## Location

Buried Giant, Hinterland

## Status

Active — old (sixty-seven), still at Buried Giant: a war veteran,
mason, and steward of the Spire's ruin, a source of first-hand history
for anyone passing through rather than family story. Born Year 473.

## Voice

Plain and precise, a tradesman's vocabulary rather than a scholar's —
measures and materials over abstractions, though he still reads well
enough to quote the old page word for word when it matters. Verbal
habit: answers a direct question by citing the page or "what's
written" before ever giving his own opinion first, and still talks
about danger and trust the same way a ledger talks about debts —
settled, unsettled, owed. Emotional default: quieter now than he was
at nineteen, less urgent, more willing to let a silence sit
unanswered. Speech rhythm: slower than it used to be, with longer
pauses before answering, though the same habit of checking his own
words against the page first is still there underneath it.
