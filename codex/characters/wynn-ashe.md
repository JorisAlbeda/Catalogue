# Wynn Ashe

## Description

Wynn Ashe is old now, sixty-seven, hands slower than they were but
still chalk-marked at the seams the way a mason's always are. He still
carries the same folded page, ink long gone soft at the creases, in
the same inside pocket out of habit more than need these days. Still
counts a debt unsettled the same way the old family stories say Iona
Ashe always did — a debt over a century old now, and by his own
reckoning still not paid, though he no longer expects to be the one
who pays it.

White hair, cropped short out of the same old practicality rather than
vanity. A weathered, deep-lined face, watchful grey eyes gone a little
paler with age but still slow to blink first, and the same thin pale
scar through one eyebrow from a chisel that slipped when he was
sixteen. Walks with a stick now, more for the bad knee than the
balance.

## History

Wynn grew up in an Ashvale tavern his family had kept for generations,
Iona Ashe's coat hanging by its hearth since long before his
grandmother's time, the tavern-song page still sewn into its lining —
several generations separated him from Iona herself, who died long
before he was born, but the family kept both the coat and her old habit
of never calling a debt settled just because it had gone quiet. His
branch had sent someone out to check on the Great Spire's memorial
stone every so often since Iona first asked for its recutting, eighty
years back; three years before the Openhold war, at sixteen and newly
apprenticed to an Ashvale mason, it was Wynn's own turn, and he spent
that summer re-tracing the worn grooves of Ressa Kindler's own words to
keep them legible — upkeep, not authorship. The Spire was never just a
monument to his family — it marked the ground where the Darkest Flame
itself was buried, and the tending visits were never really about the
inscription. Word reached the tavern the year the war began that the
tower had come down entirely, filed by Openhold's own reports as
nothing but bad weather, no one's ledger to answer for. What frightened
Wynn wasn't the ruling — it was that Buried Giant had sat in bandit
hands for years regardless, picked over by relic-hunters hoping for
exactly the kind of find that shouldn't be found, with nothing left
standing to mark what was actually underfoot. His own ancestor Iona had
tried to take this same ground once, for the same reason, and lost it
back to the Woodland Empire before she could hold it. Wynn petitioned
Openhold for leave to raise a company of Hold volunteers — mostly
Ashvale folk who knew the old stories as well as he did — and marched
them to Buried Giant himself, at nineteen, taking a copy of the coat's
own song and the tavern's own Yarrow Doss along with him. Arrived Year
492, and recruited Yarrow as his adviser there, meaning to hold this
ground outright rather than watch it from a tavern hearth three hundred
miles off.

Called the company the Cairn Watch once they'd made camp within sight
of the fallen tower. Wynn led it himself; there was no one else he
trusted to. Digging through the tower's own rubble, he recovered the
Darkest Flame itself — a debt four generations of his family had
watched over without ever once seeing its shape.

Perrine rode on Buried Giant the month after their confrontation at the
Openhold Plains, naming her terms to his face rather than sending word
ahead: the book, or a fight, that day. Wynn didn't hand it over. He put
the Cairn Watch between her and the Spire's own rubble — and only then,
watching them stand to it, did the old truth about whose ground this
always was land somewhere he couldn't argue it away any longer. It
didn't hold. She took the book back with her own hands. She didn't
touch his claim on the ground itself, or ask him to answer for anything
beyond it, exactly as she'd told him at the Openhold Plains she meant
to. He said nothing to anyone about it for a long while after.

He found the healed garden at Great Slums weeks before Tinney
Woodborne ever did, though he never told them so. When Tinney found it
too and asked him to renounce Openhold outright over it, he refused —
but he rode to Perrine that same week anyway, asking her to leave the
ground be, and told her plainly, unprompted, that the Darkest Flame at
the Keep had been his own hand and not hers. She couldn't give him the
garden; the People's Favor sat on it, and her own account with Tinney
had shrunk to a single day. Rather than fight Tinney himself or let
Perrine march in blind, he offered the one thing that cost him without
breaking either promise: going back to Tinney once more, to ask them
himself to put the banner down.

From cover with the last of his own company, he watched Perrine's own
Campaign break against that garden, watched Dunstan Reeve's spear find
Tinney and the Old Oak take what was left of them in rather than let it
end there, then rode up in time to hear Tinney's own borrowed voice
finish killing Perrine mid-retreat. Days later, in a tavern with
Yarrow, Corin, and Dagny, Teagan Sedge found him and made him give the
whole account — Tinney, Perrine, all of it, the two of them the last
of the war's own principals left standing by her own count. She asked
him plainly whether he'd light the Flame again knowing what it had
cost her own second. He said yes, and said he'd do it again on the
same terms. She named him for it — a weapon that fires the same
regardless of who's standing across from it next — and when he
reached for his hold as an excuse, turned on him outright: hundreds
dead, Openhold gone, and no sign he'd ever once stopped to consider he
might have been wrong. He said he'd tried — talked to both Tinney and
Perrine, tried to call the fight off before either one started. She
didn't take it as an answer. Left with a look more pitying than angry,
and didn't look back. He rose to go after her; Corin caught his arm,
and he let himself be stopped.

He never went home to Ashvale — not permanently. He spent the decades
since holding the ground he'd marched to take, re-cutting Ressa
Kindler's memorial words into a fresh marker each time weather or
bandits wore the last one back to nothing, the same work, over and
over. He and Teagan Sedge never spoke again, and he never wrote to her
either, though the tavern still keeps every letter he wrote and didn't
send. Yarrow, Corin, and Dagny are all gone now, each in their own
time; Wynn buried all three himself at Buried Giant rather than send
word back to Ashvale for it. He's old now — sixty-seven, walking with
a stick, slower to answer a question than he used to be — but he's
still there, still able to point out exactly where the tower stood and
tell the story straight if anyone asks him to. He made a copy of his
own folded page not long ago and left it at the tavern hearth alongside
the coat, in case a day comes when he can't tell it himself anymore.
What's remembered of him at Buried Giant so far isn't the Flame — it's
the marker, recut so many times over so many years that nobody but
Wynn himself remembers what Ressa Kindler's original letters actually
looked like.

## Location

Buried Giant, Hinterland

## Status

Active — old (sixty-seven), still at Buried Giant: a war veteran,
mason, and steward of the Spire's ruin, a source of first-hand history
for anyone passing through rather than family story. Born Year 473.

## Voice

Plain and precise, a tradesman's vocabulary rather than a scholar's —
measures and materials over abstractions, though he still reads well
enough to quote the old page word for word when it matters. Verbal
habit: answers a direct question by citing the page or "what's
written" before ever giving his own opinion first, and still talks
about danger and trust the same way a ledger talks about debts —
settled, unsettled, owed. Emotional default: quieter now than he was
at nineteen, less urgent, more willing to let a silence sit
unanswered. Speech rhythm: slower than it used to be, with longer
pauses before answering, though the same habit of checking his own
words against the page first is still there underneath it.
