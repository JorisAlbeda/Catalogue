# The Darkest Flame

## Description

The Darkest Flame, Sticky Fire, Verdflayme. It was a flame contained in a glass orb, a weapon granted to Lilianne by the Old Oak. It was designed to end conflicts by burning all remnants of disagreement, leaving nothing to fuel further strife. The relic's power required a secret to be whispered to the orb, costing its wielder dearly. It was carried by those who sought to control or end battles, often with dire consequences. Its use was tied to the balance of power, as seen in its role during the war and its eventual burial. The orb's presence symbolized both destruction and the weight of choices made in its name.

## History

The Darkest Flame was granted by the Old Oak to the First Fire Talker under the name Verdflayme, though its exact origin remains obscured. It became a weapon of influence, used to sway outcomes in conflicts by burning through opposing forces.

During the Sorcerer's War, the Sorcerer of the Woods convinced the Fire Talkers to break their bonds with Halvard Cray's Empire, and they then fought at his side, notably at the Battle for the Drowned City, where they defeated Isolde Varn's armies with Verflayme, leading to her death.

Sixty years later, the Old Oak granted the Sticky Fire to Lilianne at the start of her war against Sorrel Wick's rebellion. She used it to slaughter Sorrel Wick's forces at the Firestorm at Buried Giant. After that war ended, Lilianne chose to bury Sticky Fire, ending its cycle of destruction. The relic's final resting place became a memorial, symbolizing peace over violence. Its legacy endured in stories and superstitions, even as its physical form was lost, and it became known as the Darkest Flame.

It is said that the palace in the Deep Woods still holds the Darkest Secret, a tome that tells of how to conjure the Darkest Flame itself...

The Chancellor's line kept the tome close rather than leave it at the palace the legends still point to — Lilith Woodborne was carrying it herself at Plains, sealed behind the ring pried from the Great Spire's rubble, until Marren Wick broke her last guards there and took both the ring and the tome for her own. The tome itself was locked inside a plain chest Ivan had kept within arm's reach even at Magician's Code, mistaken then for nothing more than a keepsake box; it took the better part of an afternoon after the ring changed hands to find that chest among the Chancellor's retinue and force it open.

That account concerns the Darkest Secret, the tome — a different object from the Flame itself. The orb Lilianne buried at Buried Giant was never dug up again in the intervening decades, not by the Woodland Empire, not by Iona Ashe's own failed campaign there, and not by any of the bandits who've held the ground since: it stayed buried, exactly where she left it, for the better part of a century. This past season, Wynn Ashe — descended, several generations removed, from Iona's own line, arrived to secure the site — found it in the rubble of the fallen Great Spire while clearing ground he'd just taken from the bandits holding it, and recovered it for himself, paying its own listed cost. He knew it on sight from the family's own page before Yarrow Doss ever named it aloud: this was the thing four generations of his family had stood watch over without ever once seeing it. It now travels with him rather than sitting at any site.

The Flame and the tome were back in the same hands for one month only. Wynn broke Openhold Keep's own gate with the orb — the first time since Lilianne's own war it's been lit in anger — and carried the tome out of the same siege, taken from Teagan Sedge once the walls finally gave. He read the first page of it before Perrine Oswick followed him home to Buried Giant and took it back by the same right of arms, using the very warband Wynn had thought was his own to do it. The orb stays with him. The tome doesn't.

Sometime in the years since, it left Wynn Ashe's own hands again, and this account holds no record of exactly when or why. What's known: it turned up buried under Oathforge, at the high forge worked for three generations by the Marrow family — sunk deep enough, and long enough, that even the smiths above it never once suspected what was under their own floor. A smith's own line, driving Bandits off that same ground to reclaim it, dug it loose without meaning to and without knowing yet what it was they'd found. Cade Marrow took it for his own, the same season he renamed the mountain Oathforge.

Held long enough, it doesn't stay quiet in anyone's hand: it wants a secret paid to it, and takes more from whoever's holding it the longer they go without paying. Cade felt the demand within days of pulling it free of the ground, and paid it — silently, in secrets, not in ritual — every day since, never naming the price out loud to anyone until he was already in chains.

The mechanism itself, tested rather than merely described: the secret has to be spoken directly against the container — lips or a bare palm to jar or orb, never just thought or written down — and it has to be something never before told to another living soul; a secret already spoken once to anyone else, or already fed to the Flame on some earlier day, doesn't satisfy it, however true it still is. Payment lands as a flare — the fire inside jumps hot-white for the length of one held breath, visible even through the jar's own dark glass — and that flare is the only proof it actually took the secret; silence afterward means it was refused and the debt still stands, uncollected. A day missed without a fresh secret spoken makes the container itself run hot: warm enough by evening to notice through a sleeve, hot enough by the second missed day to raise a blister on bare skin holding it without a wrap. Cade's own left palm — already scarred from years at the forge — carries a handful of newer marks laid over the old burns, from the few days early on when he tried going without paying rather than hand over another secret to it.

Year 540. Taken from Cade Marrow at Ancient City's own gate, after the better part of a week spent hunting him across his own ground — Cade never once stood to fight the company sent after him, lighting the fire and burning a path through it instead every time the distance closed, until the company that started the chase wasn't a company by the time it reached the ruin. Carried on in a sealed jar rather than the orb it's described as above — whatever container actually holds it, the demand travels with it. The instant it changed hands, Senna Coldwell, Fia of the Ampa, and Cade himself all felt the same pull at once, unprompted: a certainty that the room could be cleared and the fire claimed outright, inside a minute, if any one of them let their body follow it. None of them did. Senna claimed the jar for her own rather than handing it back or passing it to Fia, on the read that trading hands starts its clock over — and swore to name what it asks of her out loud, to both of them, every time it asks, rather than paying it in silence the way Cade did.

The company that started that week's hunt was the better part of a full warband — near eighty men, by any honest accounting. What reached Ancient City at the end of it wasn't enough left standing to call a company anymore. Cade lit the fire fresh each time the distance closed, for the better part of a week, and never once stood to meet them with a blade instead. He named it satisfaction in his own hand before he was taken.

Fia set a condition on Senna's keeping of it, spoken plainly in front of Cade and the watching guards rather than settled privately: killing with it, under any circumstance, ends their friendship outright and marks Senna a criminal to the forest from that day on. Cade, conceding it to Senna rather than pressing his own claim further, set a narrower condition of his own — chains or not, he'll come take it back himself the day it's ever turned against Oathforge.

## Location

Currently held by Senna Coldwell, taken from Cade Marrow at Ancient City in Year 540. The tome (the Darkest Secret) was buried with Perrine Oswick, per the last account above.
