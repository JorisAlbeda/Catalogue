# Openhold Keep

## Description

An old, half-fallen tower on the open ground at the Openhold Plains, older than anyone in Marren Wick's company could account for. Long squatted in by a bandit clan before Marren cleared and reclaimed it. The keep, and the town that grew up around it, gave the whole ground its new name: Openhold.

## History

Raised by hands long forgotten, the keep stood in ruin for years, home to nothing but the bandits who'd made it theirs. In the days after Marren Wick broke Lilith Woodborne's guard at the Openhold Plains and took the Circlet of Command and the Darkest Secret, a stonemason travelling with her recognized what the ruin could still be. Marren drove the bandits out and set the mason to rebuilding it properly — walls first, then a gate — meant to shelter her own people rather than settle any old account.

When Teagan Sedge took the gate by bribe rather than siege, the gatehouse became a holding cell in all but name — Wallwatch soldiers who hadn't died defending it and hadn't taken her coin the way Ren Ashby did, left locked up with no one quite deciding what to do with them. Three months on, Perrine Oswick walked in past Teagan's own garrison and opened the door herself. Nobody stopped her.

Wynn Ashe's Cairn Watch spent weeks breaking against these same walls the following month, in a siege bigger than anything the Openlands have seen in living memory — company after company thrown at stone that held every time, until there was almost nothing left to throw. What ended it wasn't a breach at all: Wynn turned the Darkest Flame itself on the gatehouse's own defenders, and the fight that had cost him nearly everything up to that point was over within minutes. Ren Ashby and the Farrow Company holding the gate didn't survive it. The walls that had thrown back Rowan Voss's whole column, generations back, and held against Teagan's own bribe more recently, finally answered to something the mortar was never built to withstand. Wynn withdrew what little of his own company survived within days, and bandits walked through the same gate unopposed before the week was out. The keep fell into disrepair in the years that followed, becoming a monument to the founding days of Openhold.
